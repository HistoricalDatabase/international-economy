Answering the Prompt: Becoming a "Nation"

During the Jeffersonian and Jacksonian eras, the United States evolved into a cohesive nation not merely through political documents, but through economic interdependence. The Market Revolution knitted the disparate regions together into a single commercial network.

Regional Specialization

The North became a hub for manufacturing, textiles, and finance; the West emerged as the nation's agricultural breadbasket supplying grain and livestock; and the South specialized heavily in cotton production driven by slave labor to feed global textile mills.

The Rise of a Unified Identity

By relying on each other's distinct regional outputs, Americans developed a shared national consciousness. Commercial integration created a standardized national market, shifting local loyalties toward an interconnected American empire of commerce.
