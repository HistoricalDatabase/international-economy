Transport Summary

Early in the 19th century, critical shifts in American commerce were fueled by major transportation ways that connected distant regions and lowered shipping costs. Transportation impacted individual mobility and economy, allowing it to be easier to travel across different states. There were many different types of transportation methods, the following are steamboats which allowed shipping goods at good speeds on water. Canals which were man made waterways, lowered shipping cost and were directed to major ports and markets. Roads and railroads which allowed faster travel on land and pathways.

The Steamboat & The Clermont

Robert Fulton's first steamboat was the first successful creation in human history on August 17, 1807. Robert named it Clermont, and it was made for faster and reliable travel in rivers and oceans efficiently, while massive canals were under construction.

The steamboat allowed safer travel through heavy upstreams and downstreams against currents. The steamboat not only can travel through currents but through overcoming wind. Because boats back then had sails which the wind directed the boats to other directions due to the wind. And if the wind was strong it could’ve directed the boat to the wrong area.

Fulton’s primary goal for the steamboat was to create a reliable and viable water transportation. Something that can overcome river currents and can be used to expand trade and settlements.
