Infrastructural Breakthroughs

The physical unification of the United States relied heavily on sweeping infrastructure projects and mechanical inventions that drastically reduced the time and cost of shipping goods.

The Erie Canal (1825)

Financed by the state of New York, the Erie Canal connected the Great Lakes to the Hudson River. It immediately slashed shipping costs and transit times, turning New York City into the financial capital of the nation and linking Midwestern agricultural output directly to Eastern markets.

Steamboats and Railroads

Robert Fulton’s steamboat revolutionized upstream river navigation, turning rivers like the Mississippi into two-way commercial highways. Following the steamboat, the rapid expansion of railroads in the 1830s allowed goods and people to move independently of natural waterways, tightening economic ties across the Union.
