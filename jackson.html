The Jacksonian Era (1829–1837)

The Jacksonian period radically redefined what it meant to be a "nation" by shifting focus toward the common white man, expanding mass political participation, and aggressively defending federal authority against state pushback.

Key Forces Shaping the Nation

  • Expansion of Democracy: Property qualifications for voting were largely abolished for white males, shifting governance from an elite-led establishment to mass popular democracy.
  • The Nullification Crisis (1832): When South Carolina tried to nullify federal tariff laws, Andrew Jackson took a hard stance, cementing the legal principle that federal union is supreme and indestructible.
  • The Market Revolution: Canals, steamboats, and early rail networks physically tied the Northern, Southern, and Western economies together into an interdependent national grid.
