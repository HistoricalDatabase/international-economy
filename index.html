Shifting from Subsistence to Commercial Power

At the dawn of the 19th century under Jefferson, most Americans lived as isolated subsistence farmers, consuming nearly everything they produced. By the time the Jacksonian era concluded, this localized lifestyle was entirely replaced by a interconnected market economy.

Farmers no longer grew crops just to feed their families; instead, technological breakthroughs, factory systems, and an explosion of internal transportation allowed them to sell goods across state lines and international markets.
